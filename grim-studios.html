Why GRIM Studios Matters

This project should stand as one of the strongest proof points on my portfolio because it shows that I can build, operate, update, market, and improve a game experience used by real people.

From Player to Developer

GRIM Studios started from my love for Minecraft and anime, but it grew into something much more technical. I worked on custom Naruto-themed and My Hero Academia-inspired servers, building experiences where players could interact with abilities, stats, progression, and themed gameplay.

Instead of just using existing systems, I learned how to customize, extend, and balance them. I had to think like a designer, developer, server owner, marketer, and community manager at once.

Real Game Development Experience

A major part of GRIM Studios was balancing abilities and player systems the same way a game developer would. I had to update stats, adjust ability specs, test features, respond to player feedback, and optimize systems so they were fun without being unfair or too heavy on the server.

This taught me that game development is not only about cool ideas. It is about iteration, balance, testing, performance, and making sure the player experience feels good.
