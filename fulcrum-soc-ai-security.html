AI-Assisted Impossible Travel Triage

My main AI-focused project was a proof of concept for assisting analysts with Microsoft Entra ID Impossible Travel alerts. The idea was to collect the evidence an analyst normally reviews and present it to an AI layer that could organize the information into a consistent triage recommendation.

The system was designed as decision support, not autonomous remediation. The analyst remained responsible for the final decision.

Alert → evidence/context → AI analysis → schema-validated result → analyst review → approve, edit, or reject

Structured Security Analysis

Instead of asking a model for an open-ended paragraph, the prototype expected a structured result that another application could reliably display and validate.

The analysis could summarize evidence such as sign-in context, MFA information, device details, network/IP information, geolocation clues, related evidence, and missing information before proposing an analyst action.

  • Classification
  • Confidence
  • Analyst summary
  • Evidence / reasoning

Human-in-the-Loop by Design

One of the most important design choices was preventing the AI from becoming the final authority. The proof of concept did not automatically close incidents, suppress alerts, whitelist activity, or perform remediation.

The interface was built around analyst review so the AI could reduce repetitive work while still allowing a professional to approve, modify, or reject its conclusion.

Synthetic Testing and Validation

The prototype was tested with synthetic scenarios rather than publishing real customer incidents. This made it possible to evaluate the workflow, output schema, and user experience while keeping sensitive security information out of the development demonstration.

The larger lesson was that enterprise AI needs guardrails, predictable interfaces, and an explicit human review process before it should participate in security operations.

What This Experience Taught Me

Fulcrum showed me how different security work feels when the output affects a real operations team. Clear documentation, repeatable processes, evidence quality, and safe boundaries matter as much as the technical idea.

The AI project also changed how I think about automation. In security, a useful AI system does not need to replace the analyst. It can organize evidence, reduce repetitive review, and produce a consistent starting point while leaving authority with the person responsible for the incident.
